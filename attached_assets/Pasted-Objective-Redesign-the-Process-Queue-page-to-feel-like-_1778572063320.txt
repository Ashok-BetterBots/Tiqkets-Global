Objective: Redesign the Process Queue page to feel like a high-end, professional enterprise dashboard (similar to Stripe or Airbnb’s host tools). The current design is heavy on purple containers and feels visually cluttered. The goal is to maximize clarity, whitespace, and "breathability" while maintaining 100% of the current functionality.
1. General Aesthetic & Layout:
• Neutral Palette: Replace the large purple blocks and borders with a clean, neutral background (very light grey #F9FAFB or pure white).
• Strategic Color: Use the brand purple only for primary actions (like the "Call Next" button) and subtle status indicators.
• Soft Elevation: Replace thick outlines with soft, "floating" shadows and increased border-radii (12px–16px) to give the cards a modern, premium feel.
2. Header & Navigation Refinement:
• The "Call Next Customer" Bar: The current thick purple bar is too dominant. Redesign this as a refined primary action. Perhaps a crisp white bar with a bold purple button, or a slimmer, more elegant version that doesn't "crush" the rest of the UI.
• Tab System (Active, Waiting, etc.): The current pill-shaped tabs feel dated. Switch to a modern segmented control or a minimal underline-style tab system. Use high-contrast typography for the active state and a soft grey for inactive states.
3. Queue Cards (The "Next in Line" & Customer Tiles):
• Information Hierarchy: The "Position" and "Ticket Number" (T001) should be clearly legible but not distracting. Use a mono-spaced font or a bold Sans-Serif for ticket numbers to give them a "utility" feel.
• Details Section: Group the "Walk-in," "1 min waiting," and "Dine In" tags more elegantly. Use small, subtle chips/badges with light background tints instead of just icons and text.
• The "Call Next" Button: Within the card, this button should feel tactile and premium. Ensure the icon and text are perfectly balanced with plenty of internal padding.
4. Typography & Interaction:
• Font: Use Inter or SF Pro for a clean, tech-focused look.
• Spacing: Significantly increase the vertical spacing between cards and the horizontal padding within them. The screen currently feels "squeezed."
• Micro-interactions: Add a subtle "pulse" or glow to the "Next in Line" card to signify its priority. Implement smooth transitions when switching between tabs (e.g., "Waiting" to "Called").
5. Bottom Navigation:
• Clean up the bottom nav bar. Use thinner, more modern stroke icons. Ensure the "Queue" active state is indicated with a subtle dot or a slight color shift rather than a heavy change.
Summary Goal: Transform this from a "utility app" into a "premium SaaS experience." It should look like a tool designed for a high-end boutique or a modern flagship restaurant.
Tips for your Replit Agent:
• Focus on the "Position" badge: In the current screenshot, "Position 4" looks a bit like a random sticker. Tell the agent to integrate it into the card design (perhaps top-right corner with a soft grey background).
• Empty States: If you have screens for when the queue is empty, ask the agent to design a "minimalist illustration" or a clean typography-based message to match this new style.
